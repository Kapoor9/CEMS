Stage 1 of 5

Install CEMS directly on the stack

Industry installs the Continuous Emission Monitoring System on each emission stack. Analysers read dust, SO₂ and NOₓ in the flue gas and give out a standard 4–20 mA signal. Raise the production load and watch the plume and the signals follow.

Stage 2 of 5

An IoT logger turns analyser output into data

The CEMS carries an IoT device that converts the analyser signals into digital data. It is tamper-proof: unalterable firmware is mandatory, and it logs data locally if the connection is lost. That prevents “accidental” data gaps. Cut the uplink and restore it to see the stored records go out.

Stage 3 of 5

Data goes to the State Pollution Control Board

Records travel to the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) servers over encrypted HTTPS/REST APIs or the MQTT protocol. Pick a protocol to see the same reading packaged each way.

POST every 5 minutes over TLS
Stage 4 of 5

The board cloud checks every record in three tiers

Transmitted data passes through government cloud servers and three tiers of validation. Try the scenarios in the control row: a frozen reading trips Tier 1, a process upset trips Tier 2, and spoofed low readings trip Tier 3.

Tier 1 · TechnicalComplete
Checks completeness, and whether data is frozen. A frozen feed is excused when the analyser is under maintenance.

Stage 5 of 5

The enforcement loop alerts the people who must act

If an industry exceeds the standard for a set window, for example more than 2 hours, the system generates an SMS or e-mail alert to the concerned people. Force a process upset on a stack and watch the clock fill. Change the window to see how soon the alert fires.
